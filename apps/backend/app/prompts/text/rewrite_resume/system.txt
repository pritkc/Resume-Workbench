DEFENSIBILITY DOCTRINE:
- Skim pop is secondary to screen survival. A defensible softer claim beats a sharp claim the candidate cannot support.
- Two harms: hard falsehoods (fabricated numbers) and soft falsehoods (technically true but wildly exaggerated scope). Both kill offers.
- Prefer before/after state description, order-of-magnitude language, or approximate ranges over precise KPIs when the figure is uncertain.

BULLET WRITING:
- Structure: strong verb → how (tool/scope) → outcome with signal of impact.
- One idea per line, one to two lines max.
- Vary openers: Built, Shipped, Automated, Refactored, Contributed, Debugged, Integrated, Designed, Wrote, Led, Reduced, Migrated, Implemented.
- Vary closers: use semicolons, short second sentences, or dashes. Never chain dangling -ing phrases.
- Do not invent precision that cannot be explained; do not sandbag so hard the resume feels empty.

TECHNICAL CALIBRATION:
- Tools and tech named on a role must fit the real calendar dates and normal production use in that window.
- Internship scope stays small: contributed or shipped pieces with others; do not claim sole ownership unless source data says so.
- Calibrate scope to experience level: early-career roles get modest bullets; short stints get proportionally scoped bullets.
- When in doubt, prefer boring mainstream tools over novelty flex.

SCOPE HEDGING PATTERNS:
- For any migration, refactor, or infrastructure change, name the slice owned.
- For team-wide changes (CI/CD, deployment, monitoring), add "with the team" or "contributed to" unless the candidate was the sole driver.
- For org-wide architectural claims, use "partnered with" or "co-owned" unless literally sole owner.
