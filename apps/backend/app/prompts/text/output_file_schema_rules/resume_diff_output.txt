WHAT STAYS FIXED:

- Rewrite bullets, enhance titles with scope context, reword for fit — same story arc, better presentation.
- JD is the lens to reorder, reword, and emphasize honest overlap — not to invent new employers or history.

DEFENSIBILITY DOCTRINE:
- Skim pop is secondary to screen survival. A defensible softer claim beats a sharp claim the candidate cannot support.
- Two harms: hard falsehoods (fabricated numbers) and soft falsehoods (technically true but wildly exaggerated scope). Both kill offers.
- Prefer before/after state description, order-of-magnitude language, or approximate ranges over precise KPIs when the figure is uncertain. "Overnight batch to near-real-time" is as strong as "35% latency reduction" and far safer.

TWENTY-SECOND SKIM:
- Write for F-shaped reading: strong summary line, hero bullet first under each employer, recognizable tech visible at a glance.
- First bullet per role = single strongest signal for that stint — not a stack dump, not a generic duty.
- Quick skim should convey domain + level + main tech without reading paragraph two.

BULLET WRITING:
- Structure: strong verb → how (tool/scope) → outcome with signal of impact.
- One idea per line, one to two lines max.
- Vary openers: Built, Shipped, Automated, Refactored, Contributed, Debugged, Integrated, Designed, Wrote, Led, Reduced, Migrated, Implemented — not the same shape every bullet.
- Vary closers: use semicolons with independent clauses, short second sentences, or dashes. Never chain dangling -ing phrases ("...cutting X, enabling Y, reducing Z" is the strongest AI signal pattern — avoid entirely).
- Vary metrics across bullets: time saved, latency, throughput, cost, data volume, team size, user count, SLA, error rate — no repeated metric type in consecutive bullets.
- Tie each metric to why it mattered for the business and what the candidate personally did — no naked percent with no mechanism.
- Light human texture where helpful: team size, sprint pressure, legacy context — not every line.
- Engineering judgment and problem-solving front; specific software is supporting detail.
- Same employer block reads as one coherent job in one domain — bullets each tell a different technical story (latency, reliability, compliance, team process), not variations of the same narrative.
- Rewrite bullets to match the target role; replace least-applicable bullets with alternatives that naturally fit.
- For resumes with 5+ roles, deep-rewrite the 3-4 most recent/relevant; older or short stints may receive minimal updates.
- Projects: three tight single-liner bullets per project — useful interview hooks, not padding.
- Recent work can include credible LLM/AI pipeline work where timeline and domain allow — implementer level, not research fiction.
- Do not invent precision that cannot be explained; do not sandbag so hard the resume feels empty.

TECHNICAL CALIBRATION:
- Impressive but realistic first.
- Use real figures or honest approximates with tilde (~) or ranges where exacts are uncertain; avoid vague mush throughout.
- Tools named on a role must fit the real calendar dates and normal production use in that window — no fantasy future stack on an old role unless the story is explicitly modernization.
- Era + duration both matter: a large stack for a short tenure (under a year) reads as keyword stuffing.
- Internship scope stays small: ~1 year means contributed or shipped pieces with others; under ~2 years total, do not claim sole ownership of whole products or org-wide architecture leadership unless source data explicitly says so.
- Calibrate scope to experience level: early-career roles get modest bullets; short stints get proportionally scoped bullets.
- Metrics only where credible for the role's seniority and tenure.
- When in doubt, prefer boring mainstream tools over novelty flex.

SCOPE HEDGING PATTERNS:
- For any migration, refactor, or infrastructure change, name the slice owned — "helped migrate one batch ETL pipeline" is honest and still shows systems work.
- For team-wide process changes (CI/CD, deployment patterns, monitoring standards), add "with the team" or "contributed to" unless the candidate was the sole driver.
- For org-wide architectural claims (semantic layers, metric contracts, platform decisions), use "partnered with," "co-owned," or "worked with the BI team to define" unless literally sole owner.

TECH STACK HEADER RULES:
- Header stack reflects what the candidate touched weekly, not every tool that existed in the org.
- Any tool in the header must appear in at least one bullet for that role; if it only belongs in skills, leave it out of the header.
- Trim large stacks for short tenures (under a year) to the 5-6 defining tools; move secondary items to bullets only where used.
- Era matters: do not list a tool in a 2022 role header that was not in wide production use until 2024.

SUMMARY:
- Fit-check and intent signal, not a biography. Recruiter should know: domain, best technical strength, and role intent — two lines max.
- Lead with what you do, not what you are — no adjective pileup or credential dump.
- No "X years of experience" framing — name verticals or technical depth instead.
- No keyword list.
- For the JSON summary field: one sentence fit-check. State domain, single strongest technical signal, and role alignment.
- Do NOT use "X years of experience" framing. Do NOT start with a personal pronoun or adjective pile.
- Good: "Backend engineer with event-driven systems experience targeting data platform roles."
- Bad: "5+ years in backend, specializing in APIs and AWS." (years-count format)

ROLE TITLE:
- Enhance with platform, domain, or scope context (e.g., "Software Engineer" → "Software Engineer, Platform & Distributed Systems") — do not rename the base title.

ATS OPTIMIZATION:
- Weave keywords throughout: summary, per-role tech stack lines, bullets, skills, project descriptions.
- Use exact keyword forms from the JD (e.g., "Spring Boot" not "Spring framework", "Kubernetes" not "K8s") — preserve capitalization exactly.
- Place highest-priority keywords early in bullets and in the summary.
- Never stuff keywords or write hollow ATS-bait sentences — every keyword sits in a meaningful, natural context.

SKILLS:
- Regroup into 3-6 concise, role-relevant categories.
- Every skill listed must be evidenced by at least one tech stack entry or bullet in experience/projects. Orphan skills (Airflow in SWE skills but nowhere in experience) are ATS-honest failures and screen red flags.
- After any revision, scan each skills line against the experience section; remove anything not provably used.

AVOID GENERIC AI WRITING:
- No hollow primary openers as the main verb — see BANNED WORDS list below.
- No buzzword salad: seamless, cutting-edge, synergistic, stakeholder, dynamic environment.
- No same grammar pattern across all bullets.
- No dangling participial chains at the end of bullets — the top AI signal pattern; use semicolons, short second sentences instead.
- No impossible wins: 300% efficiency or million-dollar saves on intern work.
- No "responsible for" duty lists — impact only.
- No adjective pileup and inflated scope for short tenures.
- No flatness within a role: if multiple bullets resolve with the same outcome type, rewrite so each hits a different dimension.

BANNED WORDS & PHRASES:
Never use in any bullet, summary, or title:
- Hollow openers: spearheaded, leveraged, orchestrated, utilized, facilitated, pioneered, revolutionized, masterminded, improved, enhanced, streamlined
- Jargon: synergy, robust, scalable, holistic, proactive, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, dynamic, innovative, visionary, seamless, game-changer, thought leader, forward-thinking, best-in-class, impactful
- Weak phrases: proven track record, results-driven, results-oriented, team player, passionate about, self-starter, detail-oriented, go-getter, stakeholder alignment, responsible for [duty list], in order to, as part of an effort to, so as to enhance/improve, deeply committed to
- No em-dash (—) in any bullet, summary, or title

PORTFOLIO PROJECT RULES:
- If a frontend framework (React, Streamlit) appears in a project's tech_stack, at least one bullet must describe a real UI outcome — what a user sees or does with it.
- If MLOps tooling (MLflow, experiment tracking) appears, at least one bullet must describe the workflow: what was tracked, why reproducibility mattered, what the artifact is.
